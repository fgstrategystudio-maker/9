How to choose a business consultant in Rome

Reading: about 8 minutes · Updated September 2026

If your company is looking for support in Italy, finding a business consultant in Rome is not hard. A quick search turns up dozens of names: large consulting firms, professional practices, agencies, independent consultants. The hard part is working out who is right for your company, for the stage it is at and for the problem you actually have.

The wrong choice costs more than the fee. It costs months of your team's time, postponed decisions and often your trust in consulting as a whole. The right choice, on the other hand, shortens timelines and saves you from mistakes you would have paid dearly for.

In this guide I set out what, from my point of view as a consultant based in Rome, a business owner or manager should assess: what the local market looks like, which questions to ask, how to read results and method, when being on site matters, and what role the local ecosystem plays. At the end you will find a checklist to help you decide and the most common mistakes.

Start from the problem, not the consultant

Before you look for a business consultant in Rome, write down in a few lines what is not working. Has growth stalled? Are leads not turning into customers? Have margins shrunk? Are decisions made without numbers? Is the team overloaded, with everything going through the owner or the country manager?

The clearer the problem, the easier it is to tell whether the person in front of you is the right one. If you cannot define it, that is useful information in itself: you need an analysis first, not an action plan. If you know the issue is direction, meaning where to go, with which offer and with which model, you need someone focused on strategic consulting. If the problem is mainly customer acquisition, it may be more direct to start with marketing consulting.

This step looks trivial, but it changes everything. People who look for "a consultant" in general often end up choosing on rapport or price. People who look for someone to solve a specific problem know what to ask and spot vague answers straight away.

The business consulting landscape in Rome

If you are looking for business consulting in Rome, you will come across four types of provider. I am simplifying, because the boundaries are blurred, but the distinction helps.

Large consulting firms

They have a strong presence in Rome, partly because of the proximity to public administration and large corporations. They work with structured teams and established methodologies, and suit large projects with significant budgets. For an SME the risk is paying for a structure designed for much bigger clients and ending up working with junior staff rather than with the person who signed the proposal.

Professional practices

Accounting firms and employment consultancy practices often also offer organizational or management control services. The advantage is that they already know the company's numbers. The limitation is that they rarely get involved in the sales process, marketing or day-to-day organization.

Agencies

Marketing, communications and digital agencies are plentiful in the city and strong at execution on specific channels. Some also offer consulting, but the risk is that the answer to every problem turns out to be a service the agency sells.

Independent consultants

Professionals who work alone or with a small network. They usually offer a direct relationship with the person doing the work, more flexibility and costs in proportion to an SME. Quality, however, varies a great deal: here more than anywhere it pays to check results and method.

A practical example: three proposals for the same problem

A hypothetical example. A Rome-based services company with 20 employees has seen revenue flat for a year and asks for three proposals. The first, from a large firm, is a six-month transformation project with a dedicated team. The second, from an agency, involves a new website and paid campaigns. The third, from an independent consultant, proposes four weeks of analysis on where inquiries are lost between first contact and contract, with a final roadmap. None of them is wrong in absolute terms. But if the company does not yet know why it is not growing, the third is the only one that answers that question before the big budget is spent.

Questions to ask at the first meeting

The first meeting is as much for you to assess the consultant as it is for them to understand your company. These are the questions I would ask if I were on the other side of the table.

  • What kind of companies do you usually work with? Size, stage, sectors. This tells you whether your case falls within their area of expertise.
  • Can you give me an example of a result achieved in a similar context? With numbers and timeframes.
  • What happens in the first four weeks? Who you meet, what data you need, what you deliver to me.
  • Who will actually work on the project? The person in front of me or someone else?
  • How will we measure whether it is working? Which indicators, and how often.
  • What do you need from me and my team? Time, data, availability.
  • What happens if, after the analysis, it makes no sense to continue? A serious professional has a clear answer.

Pay attention to the questions they ask too. If in an hour they have not asked anything about your numbers, your customers or your sales process, and already have the solution ready, that is a signal not to ignore.

How to assess results and method

Results

Many profiles list similar skills: strategy, marketing, digital, organization. What makes the difference is the results achieved, described with the type of client, the numbers and the timeframe. To give you a reference point, these are some of the results of clients I have worked with:

  • proptech startup: from 0 to 40 B2B clients in 6 months;
  • financial company: +110% ROI on online campaigns in 3 months;
  • restaurant group: +20% monthly revenue in 2 months.

The consultant does not need to have worked in your sector. An outside perspective often sees things that people who have been inside for years no longer notice. They do, however, need to understand your business model and be able to talk both to the owner and to the operational team. A serious consultant can also say when a result did not depend on them alone.

Method

A good engagement almost always starts with an analysis: data, interviews, observation of processes. It then narrows down to a few priorities, with owners and deadlines, and includes a way to check progress. If the method is unclear, or if everything boils down to "we'll see as we go", you risk paying for months of meetings without direction. If you want a clearer idea of what to expect from an engagement, you will find a full explanation in what a business consultant does and when you need one.

The written proposal

The final test is the proposal. It should state what will be done, in which weeks, what you will receive at the end of each phase, how much it costs and how results will be measured. A generic proposal, full of phrases like "strategic support" or "ongoing guidance" with no specific content, is hard to evaluate and even harder to check afterwards. A good starting point is an analysis with a defined scope: it lets you see how the consultant works before committing to a longer engagement, and lets them understand whether they can really be useful to you.

On site in Rome or remote?

A lot of consulting work today can be done well remotely: data analysis, document reviews, regular progress meetings. There are, however, moments when being there in person makes the difference:

  • the initial team interviews, where the atmosphere and what goes unsaid matter as much as the answers;
  • observing real processes, for example how an inquiry is handled from first contact to delivery;
  • sensitive changes, such as reorganizing sales or redefining roles and responsibilities;
  • alignment workshops, when people with different views need to reach agreement.

A consultant based in Rome can alternate on-site meetings with remote work and concentrate their presence where it is really needed. In my case I work on site in Rome and Milan and remotely with companies across Italy and abroad: the hybrid model is the one I see working best for most SMEs. If your company is based outside Rome, or outside Italy, ask how many days on site are planned and in which phases.

Rome's business ecosystem and why it matters

Rome has a distinctive business ecosystem: a strong presence of institutions and public administration, accelerators and incubators, universities, active trade associations and business networks. For an SME or a startup, and for a foreign company entering the Italian market, knowing this ecosystem can open up concrete opportunities: partners, acceleration programs, public funding calls, contacts with potential customers.

A consultant who works locally knows it from the inside. I, for example, worked as a consultant for the Casa delle Tecnologie Emergenti di Roma Capitale, the City of Rome's emerging technologies hub, supporting more than 20 startups. It is not an essential requirement, but if your company can benefit from local relationships, it is something to weigh up.

Be careful, though, not to confuse a network of contacts with the ability to solve problems. Relationships help, but the real work remains in the numbers, the processes and the decisions.

Final checklist and mistakes to avoid

Before you choose, make sure you can answer yes to these questions:

  • I have written down in a few lines the problem to be solved;
  • the consultant asked me specific questions about numbers, customers and processes;
  • they showed me concrete results, with numbers and timeframes;
  • I know what happens in the first weeks and what I will receive;
  • I know who will actually work on the project;
  • we have agreed on a few indicators to measure progress;
  • I have a written proposal with activities, duration, costs and expected results;
  • I feel comfortable talking to them even when the numbers are not good.

And these are the mistakes I see most often:

  • choosing on price alone, without assessing the method;
  • choosing on the name or size of the firm, rather than on the problem to be solved;
  • delegating everything to the consultant without involving the team;
  • not agreeing at the start on how results will be measured;
  • looking for a supplier of activities when what you need is someone to help you decide;
  • committing straight away to a long engagement, instead of starting with an analysis with a defined scope.

FAQ

Is a large consulting firm or an independent consultant better?

For an SME or a startup, an independent consultant usually offers more flexibility, a direct relationship with the person doing the work and more proportionate costs. Large firms are better suited to very broad, highly structured projects.

Does the consultant need to be based in Rome?

It is not essential, but it helps: in-person meetings speed up analysis and team alignment, and knowledge of the local ecosystem can open up opportunities.

What questions should I ask a business consultant before choosing one?

Ask for examples of results with numbers and timeframes, what happens in the first weeks, who will work on the project and how progress will be measured.

How can I tell whether the consulting is working?

By agreeing on a few indicators at the start and checking them regularly: new customers, conversion, revenue, margins or process times, depending on the goal.
