The audit

A complete analysis, with a practical outcome.

A business audit is an analysis of how the company works: how it generates clients, how it sells, how it delivers its service, how it measures results. A process audit goes into the detail of workflows, to find the steps that slow things down, are duplicated or depend on a single person.

I work through interviews with the team, a review of the available data (CRM, management software, analytics, income statements) and observation of the real processes, not the ones written down in the procedures.

The outcome is not a document to be filed away: it is a map of the bottlenecks and a ranked list of priorities, with what to do in the next 90 days and how to measure it. If the analysis only concerns marketing, there is also a marketing audit.

FAQ

Answers to the most common questions.

What is the difference between a business audit and a marketing audit?

A marketing audit analyzes channels, messaging and funnel. A business audit looks at the whole company: business model, sales, operational processes and data. If it is not clear where the problem lies, it is best to start with the business audit.

How long does a business process audit take?

It depends on the size of the company and the areas involved. For an SME it usually takes a few weeks, between interviews, data analysis and presenting the findings.

What does the company need to provide?

A few hours of the team's time for interviews and access to the available data: CRM, management software, analytics, financial reports. If the data is incomplete, we sort it out during the audit.

What happens after the audit?

You can put the roadmap into practice with your own team, or I can support you through execution with ongoing consulting.

Is an audit useful even if the company is growing?

Yes: it is often during growth phases that processes stop holding up. The audit brings the limits to light before they turn into costs.
