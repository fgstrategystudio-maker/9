Relocating to Italy

Moving to Italy: a life in nature, 30 minutes from Rome

How to have Rome close by and hills, olive trees and quiet all around: practical notes for people moving from abroad.

Updated 29 October 2026

Many people dream of moving to Italy: the food, the light, the history, a slower rhythm of life. Rome is often the first choice, but life in the city centre means traffic, small apartments and noise. There is another way: living in the countryside just north of Rome, with hills, olive groves and woods around you, and the Colosseum 30 minutes away.

Why the countryside north of Rome

  • Close to everything: the ancient Via Flaminia and the Rome ring road take you to the centre in 30–40 minutes. A local railway runs to Piazzale Flaminio, next to Piazza del Popolo.
  • Nature: Veio Regional Park, the Tiber valley and Lake Bracciano are all nearby.
  • History on your doorstep: the Arch of Malborghetto, linked to Emperor Constantine, and the Villa of Livia, wife of Augustus.
  • International: Rome Fiumicino airport is about 50 km away, and northern Rome has international schools, hospitals and services.
  • Climate: mild winters, long springs and outdoor dining from April to October.

Buying property in Italy

  1. Get an Italian tax code (codice fiscale), available from an Italian consulate or tax office.
  2. Make an offer: a written purchase proposal, often with a small deposit.
  3. Sign the preliminary contract (compromesso) with a larger deposit.
  4. Complete the deed (rogito) before a notary, who checks the property and registers the sale.

Foreigners can buy property in Italy: EU citizens on the same terms as Italians, and most others under reciprocity agreements. When farmland is involved, neighbouring farmers may have a legal right of first refusal, which the notary handles.

Residency and taxes

  • EU citizens can move freely and register as residents.
  • Non-EU citizens can apply for visas such as the elective residence visa (for people with independent income), the digital nomad visa (for remote workers) or visas for investors and entrepreneurs.
  • Tax: Italy offers special tax regimes for people who move their tax residence here, depending on personal circumstances. Speak to an adviser before you move.

A house, or an estate?
